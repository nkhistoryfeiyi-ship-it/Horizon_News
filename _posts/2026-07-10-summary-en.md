The EU Parliament has approved the 'Chat Control' regulation, allowing mass scanning of private messages by major tech platforms despite opposition from a majority of MEPs. The motion to reject the law failed because it did not secure the required absolute majority of 361 votes out of all members. This decision permits the scanning of end-to-end encrypted messages on platforms like Instagram, Discord, and Gmail without a warrant, significantly impacting user privacy and digital security standards across Europe. It sets a precedent for how the EU balances child protection measures with fundamental rights to private communication. The regulation relies on client-side scanning technology that analyzes content on user devices before encryption is applied. Voting took place on July 7, 2026, where 314 MEPs voted against the measure but fell short of the 361 votes needed to block it by default.

**Background**: End-to-end encryption (E2EE) ensures that only communicating users can read the messages, preventing third parties including service providers from accessing them. Client-side scanning involves checking files on the user's device before they are encrypted, which critics argue undermines the security guarantees of E2EE. The EU previously passed a temporary derogation in 2021 allowing such searches for child sexual abuse material (CSAM), and this recent vote extends those powers.

An opinion piece in the South China Morning Post argues that the geopolitical competition between the US and China in artificial intelligence is fundamentally driven by a race for electrical power capacity. This perspective highlights energy infrastructure as the critical bottleneck in the global AI arms race. This shift in focus underscores that AI development is no longer limited by algorithms or chips alone, but by the physical availability of electricity and grid connectivity. It impacts national security strategies, as securing reliable power sources becomes essential for maintaining technological supremacy. Recent forecasts predict AI will consume over half of data center electricity by 2028, with US data center power demand projected to more than double between 2026 and 2030. The primary constraint is now grid connectivity rather than generation capacity, driving hyperscalers to secure carbon-free and on-site power.

**Background**: Artificial intelligence workloads, particularly large-scale model training and inference, are significantly more energy-intensive than traditional computing tasks. As compute density increases, the demand for stable, high-capacity power grids has become a limiting factor for scaling AI infrastructure globally.
