Scientists from the Hefei Institutes of Physical Science and the Ningbo Institute of Materials Technology and Engineering, both under the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS), have developed a breakthrough Hall-effect magnetic sensor capable of detecting submarines at depths of up to 500 meters. This advancement bridges military-grade magnetic anomaly detection technology with consumer wearable devices, potentially transforming anti-submarine warfare capabilities while also opening new possibilities for civilian maritime applications. The Hall-effect sensor works by converting magnetic fields into measurable voltage signals, allowing it to detect minute disturbances in the Earth's magnetic field caused by a submarine's ferromagnetic hull. The CAS team's innovation centers on enhancing the sensor's sensitivity to make such detection feasible from compact, wearable form factors.

rss · South China Morning Post · Aug 15, 03:00

**Background**: Magnetic anomaly detection (MAD) is a long-established military technology that identifies submerged submarines by sensing disturbances they create in the Earth's natural magnetic field. A submarine's large ferromagnetic hull distorts the local magnetic field, which specialized magnetometers can detect. Traditional MAD systems are bulky and mounted on aircraft or ships, but miniaturized Hall-effect sensors could bring this capability to much smaller platforms, including wearable devices.
