## [Is it legal to train AI models on copyrighted books? It’s complicated](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/23/is-it-legal-to-train-ai-models-on-copyrighted-books-its-complicated/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Most published authors have had their copyrighted works used without knowledge or consent to train AI models, raising ongoing legal questions about whether this practice constitutes copyright infringement or falls under fair use exceptions. This legal debate is critical because it will determine whether AI companies can continue using copyrighted books as training data, directly impacting both the AI development industry and authors' livelihoods. Courts have historically applied the fair use doctrine to new technologies involving intermediate copying, and precedents like Authors Guild v. Google and Authors Guild v. HathiTrust have ruled that text and data mining for research purposes constitutes fair use.

rss · TechCrunch · Aug 23, 15:00

**Background**: The fair use doctrine is a legal principle that permits limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching, scholarship, or research. Text and data mining (TDM) involves copying copyrighted works to analyze patterns and extract information computationally. U.S. appellate courts have previously ruled that TDM for research purposes qualifies as fair use, though the application to generative AI training remains legally contested.
