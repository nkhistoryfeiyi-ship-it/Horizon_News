## [MIT Engineers Create Miniature Ingestible Temperature Sensor](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/25/1140952/taking-your-temperature-from-the-inside/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

MIT engineers have developed a miniature ingestible temperature sensor measuring just 6 by 4 millimeters that can continuously monitor core body temperature. The device overcomes the size and safety limitations of existing ingestible sensors, which are often too large to swallow comfortably and risk obstructing the gastrointestinal tract. This breakthrough addresses a longstanding clinical problem: oral and forehead thermometers cannot accurately capture core body temperature, while existing ingestible sensors are impractically large. The miniaturized design opens the door for safer, more comfortable continuous temperature monitoring in medical and athletic applications. The sensor achieves an accuracy of 0.01°C and transmits temperature data once per second using ultra-high-frequency radio wave modulation. An external antenna emits radio waves that are modulated by the tiny internal antenna, which then sends the signal back for the external antenna to interpret and calculate the temperature value.

rss · MIT Technology Review · Aug 25, 21:00

**Background**: Ingestible telemetric temperature sensors, often called "radio pills," have been described for over 45 years. These devices contain a temperature-sensitive quartz crystal oscillator and transmit gastrointestinal temperature data via radio waves to an external receiver. While they have gained widespread use in exercise and sports science applications, their larger size has limited broader clinical adoption due to swallowing difficulty and potential GI obstruction risks.

## [The Tooltip Delay Pattern: Preventing Flicker While Maintaining Smooth Navigation](https://blog.master.dev/tooltips-need-a-delay-and-then-they-need-to-skip-it/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The article explores a tooltip delay pattern where a brief delay prevents flickering on hover, but the delay is skipped when moving between adjacent elements for a smoother user experience. This pattern addresses a common usability issue in interactive design where tooltips either flicker annoyingly or appear too slowly, impacting user efficiency and satisfaction across web and desktop applications. The technique uses a delay threshold that prevents premature tooltip display on hover, but employs hysteresis-like logic to skip the delay when transitioning between adjacent interactive elements.

hackernews · ibobev · Aug 25, 16:35 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49436786)

**Background**: Tooltips are small pop-up labels that appear when users hover over interface elements. The delay pattern is a well-known UX technique that balances responsiveness with stability—without a delay, tooltips flicker as the mouse moves in and out of the trigger area. The concept of hysteresis, borrowed from engineering, describes systems where the output depends not just on current input but also on previous states, which is exactly how this tooltip behavior works.

**Discussion**: Community members noted that Apple under Jef Raskin implemented similar solutions in System 6 around 1993, with one commenter framing the technique as an example of hysteresis. Others shared practical experiences, including issues with Visual Studio's hover popups and a similar implementation from two decades ago.
