## [First atmosphere found on Earth-like planet in habitable zone of distant star](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy4kdd1e0ejo) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Astronomers have successfully detected an atmosphere on LHS 1140b, marking the first confirmation of an atmosphere on an Earth-sized planet located within the habitable zone of a distant star. This breakthrough was achieved using transmission spectroscopy, ruling out the possibility that the planet is a stripped mini-Neptune. This discovery is significant because it proves that rocky planets in the habitable zone can retain substantial atmospheres despite the intense stellar radiation from red dwarf stars. It validates the potential for such worlds to support liquid water and possibly life, guiding future searches for biosignatures. The detection relies on analyzing how starlight filters through the planet's atmosphere during transit, revealing molecular signatures. LHS 1140b orbits a red dwarf 48 light-years away, and previous concerns about atmospheric stripping were addressed by emission spectroscopy data.

**Background**: The habitable zone is the region around a star where conditions might allow liquid water to exist on a planet's surface. Red dwarfs are common but often emit powerful flares that can strip away planetary atmospheres, making the retention of air on nearby rocky planets a key question in astrobiology. Detecting these atmospheres requires sensitive instruments like the James Webb Space Telescope to analyze spectral lines.
