## [US Bans Differential Privacy and Noise Infusion in Census Data](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=9902) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On June 4, 2026, the U.S. Department of Commerce issued Directive DAO-216-26, which bans the use of noise infusion and differential privacy techniques in statistical products published by the Census Bureau. The directive restricts disclosure avoidance methods exclusively to 'coarsening,' effectively prohibiting the addition of random noise to datasets. This policy shift significantly impacts data science and civil liberties by removing robust mathematical guarantees for individual privacy in official government statistics. It raises concerns about potential data leaks and reduces the reliability of public data for researchers and policymakers who depend on accurate, privacy-preserving aggregates. The directive specifically forbids 'noise infusion,' defined as methods that modify a dataset by adding random values or noise, which are core components of differential privacy frameworks. While 'coarsening' remains permitted, it offers weaker privacy protections compared to the mathematically rigorous noise-based approaches previously utilized.

hackernews · flowercalled · Jul 3, 00:01 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48768992)

**Background**: Differential privacy is a framework designed to ensure that statistical analyses do not compromise the privacy of individual data entries by introducing controlled randomness. Noise infusion is a specific technique within this field that perturbs data values before publication to prevent re-identification. The Census Bureau has historically explored these methods to balance data utility with the confidentiality of sensitive economic and demographic information.
