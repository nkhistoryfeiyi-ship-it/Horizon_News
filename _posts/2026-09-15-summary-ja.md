## [A new solar cell could generate electricity underwater](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/09/a-new-solar-cell-could-generate-electricity-underwater/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Researchers have developed a new solar cell that can generate electricity underwater by solving the long-standing durability problem of perovskite materials in submerged environments. The key innovation lies not in using perovskites themselves, but in engineering a solution that allows them to last underwater. This breakthrough could enable solar-powered devices to operate in underwater environments, opening possibilities for underwater vehicles, ocean monitoring equipment, and subsea energy systems. It represents a meaningful incremental advance in making perovskite solar cells viable for real-world applications beyond land-based installations. The breakthrough centers on overcoming the moisture sensitivity that has historically plagued perovskite solar cells, which typically degrade rapidly when exposed to water. While the excerpt provides limited technical specifics, the core achievement is a durability solution rather than a new perovskite formulation.

rss · Ars Technica · Sep 14, 18:03

**Background**: Perovskite solar cells are a promising next-generation photovoltaic technology known for their high conversion efficiency and low manufacturing costs compared to traditional silicon cells. However, their commercialization has been hindered by poor long-term stability, especially when exposed to moisture, oxygen, and UV light. Making perovskites water-resistant has been one of the key challenges researchers have been working to solve for years.
