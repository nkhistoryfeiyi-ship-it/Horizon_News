## [I accidentally logged hundreds of thousands of phone calls to military bases](https://lina.sh/blog/hijacking-e164-arpa) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The author accidentally discovered that e164.arpa ENUM queries continue to route hundreds of thousands of phone calls to military bases, revealing how legacy telephony infrastructure persists with surprising security implications. This discovery highlights how forgotten legacy protocols can remain active and expose critical infrastructure, including military communications, to unintended access. It underscores a broader pattern where abandoned but not decommissioned systems create lingering security risks. ENUM works by reversing E.164 phone number digits, separating them with dots, and appending .e164.arpa to query DNS for routing information. The author stumbled upon the military routing while logging queries, not through intentional probing.

hackernews · gavide · Aug 21, 13:11 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49387570)

**Background**: ENUM (Electronic Number Mapping) was proposed in the early 2000s as a method to map traditional telephone numbers to Internet protocols, enabling VoIP systems to route calls over IP networks instead of the public switched telephone network. The e164.arpa domain stores DNS records that translate phone numbers into SIP URIs or other internet addresses. Despite being largely abandoned in favor of modern VoIP solutions, fragments of this infrastructure remain active and publicly accessible.
