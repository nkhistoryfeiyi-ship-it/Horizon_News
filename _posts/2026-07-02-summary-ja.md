Researchers have successfully created the 'SpudCell,' a synthetic cell built entirely from non-living chemicals that can eat, grow, and divide. Led by Dr. Kate Adamala, this achievement marks the first time a lab-created cell has completed a full life cycle including reproduction. This breakthrough represents a historic milestone in synthetic biology by demonstrating that life-like processes can emerge from simple chemical components without relying on existing biological templates. It opens new possibilities for understanding the fundamental origins of life and creating custom organisms for medical and industrial applications. Unlike previous attempts that modified existing cells, SpudCell was assembled from scratch using lipids, proteins, and genetic material. The team bypassed the complex natural cytoskeleton typically required for division, allowing the cell to split and pass on genetic material to future generations.
