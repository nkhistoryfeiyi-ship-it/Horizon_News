Researchers from the People’s Public Security University of China have developed a method to identify active smartphone applications and user actions by analyzing faint low-frequency electromagnetic radiation emitted by devices. This technique works even when the phone is offline, in flight mode, or encrypted. The findings were published in the peer-reviewed journal Radioengineering. This research highlights a critical vulnerability in mobile security, demonstrating that side-channel attacks can bypass standard privacy controls like encryption and offline modes. It raises significant concerns for user privacy and national security, as it allows potential surveillance without direct network access or physical possession of the device. The attack relies on amplitude-modulated electromagnetic emanations from mixed-signal circuits, which do not require close physical access to the victim device. This distinguishes it from traditional power analysis or near-field electromagnetic analysis, making it a more remote and covert threat vector.

rss · South China Morning Post · Jul 7, 12:00

**Background**: Side-channel attacks exploit information gained from the physical implementation of a cryptosystem rather than weaknesses in the algorithm itself. Electromagnetic attacks, a specific type of side-channel attack, measure radiation emitted by electronic devices to infer internal states or extract keys. Modern smartphones emit non-ionizing radiation across various frequencies, which can theoretically be analyzed to reveal operational patterns.
